Short lesson, examples, and practice problems for young learners

Short lesson: How to add numbers up to 15

Pick the strategy below that you like best, you do not have to use them all

  • Counting on: Start at the bigger number, then count forward by the smaller number.
    Example: 9 + 3 → start at 9, say “10, 11, 12” → answer is 12.
  • Make 10: Break the second number to reach 10 first, then add the rest.
    Example: 8 + 5 → 8 + 2 = 10, 10 + 3 = 13.
  • Doubles & near doubles: Use facts like 6 + 6 = 12; if it’s close, adjust by 1.
    Example: 6 + 7 → 6 + 6 = 12, then add 1 → 13.
  • Number line jumps: Draw a line, start at the first number, make small jumps for the second number.
    Example: 4 + 9 → start at 4, jump 6 to reach 10, then jump 3 → 13.

Tip: For sums up to 15, it’s fastest to start at the larger number and “count on” or “make 10.”

Teacher/Parent notes

  • Skill focus: Fluency with sums to 15, decomposing to 10, doubles/near‑doubles.
  • Use manipulatives: Counters or number lines help visualize “make 10” jumps.
  • Differentiate: Swap numbers to adjust difficulty; keep totals ≤ 15 to match the goal.
