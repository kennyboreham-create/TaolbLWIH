The 5 Mistakes I Made When Creating My Brand

Posted on November 17, 2025 · Category: Author Journey

When I first set out to create The Adventures of Lukey Buke, a children’s book I published on Amazon, I had no idea how much of a learning curve I was about to face. I poured my heart into the first book, then pushed forward to release a second and even an activity book. That year was full of ups and downs a crash course in publishing, branding, and perseverance.

One thing I learned quickly: if you don’t have quality teachers or mentors guiding you, you risk ending up with garbage. And trust me, I made plenty of mistakes along the way. Here are five of the biggest ones I made while building my brand.

5. Grammar Check

I thought I was covered by asking a couple of friends to proofread and running my manuscript through Grammarly. But errors still slipped through into the published version. Readers notice these things, and it doesn’t leave a good impression. Lesson learned, invest in a professional editor.

4. Illustrations

I pieced together basic website images and even ran a contest for kids to submit drawings. While fun, the final product didn’t have the polished, professional look that customers expect. Illustrations are the heartbeat of a children’s book cutting corners here cost me in terms of brand perception.

3. Paid Ads Too Early

I jumped into paid ads before my brand was even established. That was a mistake. There are so many free ways to build your base, social media engagement, email lists, networking before you spend money. Paid ads only work when you already have a strong foundation.

2. Website Design

When I created my first website, I chose colors and designs that I thought were cool. The problem? I didn’t think about what my customers wanted. A website isn’t about your taste, it’s about clarity, usability, and serving your audience.

1. Obsessing Over Bad Reviews

When negative reviews came in, I let them get to me. Instead of fixing the issues reviewers pointed out, I stopped working on the project for a while. Reviews can sting, but they’re also free feedback. The best move is to use them as a roadmap for improvement.

Conclusion

Every mistake taught me something valuable. Building a brand isn’t about perfection, it’s about persistence, learning, and adapting. If you’re starting your own creative journey, I hope my missteps help you avoid a few pitfalls.

Build the brand you want and don’t stop.
